Most organizations our size fill a page like this quickly. A photograph, a first name, a difficult situation, a hopeful ending. It works  people give. We have the stories. What we do not yet have is consent we are confident in. Real consent is harder than a signature. It means the person understands the story will be permanently public and searchable. It means they know they can say no and still receive exactly the same support. It means asking again later, because someone who agreed during a crisis may feel differently about it a year on. For a person living with HIV in Nepal, a story on the internet can affect their housing, their work, and how their family is treated. That is not a risk we will take on someone else's behalf to make our fundraising easier.So this page stays empty until we get it right.When stories do appear here, they will follow rules we have set for ourselves. Nobody's support will ever depend on agreeing to be photographed or named. Anyone can withdraw at any time and we will take the story down without asking why. People will read their own story before it is published and can change or remove any part of it. No child will be identified. And we will not publish the moment of someone's worst difficulty as though it were the point of their life.In the meantime, we would rather be judged on our programs and our accounts than on stories.
